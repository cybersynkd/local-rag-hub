# Managing Debt Used for Investments

This guide is for people considering or already using borrowed money—especially debt secured by investment or crypto assets. Borrowing to invest can magnify losses as well as gains. **A plan to repay the debt should not depend on the investment recovering in value.**

This is general educational information, not financial, tax, or legal advice.

## Before borrowing

- Know the interest rate, fees, repayment schedule, maturity date, and whether the lender can demand early repayment.
- Understand exactly what is pledged as collateral and what triggers a margin call or liquidation.
- Stress-test the plan against a sharp, sustained drop in collateral value, higher interest costs, and loss of income.
- Decide how you would repay the debt without selling the investment. Identify a reliable repayment source, such as income or cash savings.
- Keep an emergency fund separate from both the investment and the collateral. Do not count volatile assets as cash reserves.
- Borrow only an amount you could realistically repay if the investment fell substantially or became difficult to sell.
- Consider whether the plan still makes sense without leverage.

## While the debt is outstanding

- Track the balance, interest, payment dates, collateral requirements, and lender notices.
- Make required payments from dependable funds; avoid relying on new borrowing or investment gains to cover them.
- Keep enough readily available funds to handle scheduled payments and a possible collateral shortfall.
- Avoid adding debt or pledging additional assets just to maintain a position.
- Reassess the plan when your income, loan terms, collateral value, or risk tolerance changes.

## If collateral value falls or repayment becomes difficult

1. Read the loan and collateral terms and contact the lender promptly.
2. Confirm the amount due, deadlines, and consequences of missing a payment or collateral requirement.
3. Review realistic repayment options, including paying down the balance or reducing exposure. Do not assume you will be able to hold every position.
4. Avoid rushed decisions based on promises of a rebound or guaranteed returns.
5. Seek qualified financial, tax, or legal advice when the amounts or consequences are significant.

## Important risks

Crypto assets and other investments can lose value rapidly, trade around the clock, or become difficult to sell. A lender may liquidate collateral under the agreement, potentially at an unfavorable time. You may still owe money after collateral is sold. Borrowing costs can continue even while an investment is down, and tax consequences may apply.

**A repayment plan reduces risk; it does not eliminate it. Never borrow an amount you cannot repay if your investment falls or is forcibly sold.**
